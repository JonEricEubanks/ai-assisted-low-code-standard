# Clarification Question Bank

Questions are tiered by consequence, not by topic.

| Tier | Meaning | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Blocking. The answer changes the architecture, cost, or legal position. | Cannot mark Ready |
| Tier 2 | Shaping. The answer changes design or effort but not direction. | Can proceed with a recorded assumption |
| Tier 3 | Refining. The answer affects polish or sequencing. | Resolve during build |

## Discipline rule

Send no more than **eight Tier 1 questions** in one round. Long question lists produce no answers. Ask what blocks the build, get answers, then ask the next round.

Route every question to a role, not to "the client." Unrouted questions do not get answered.

---

## Tier 1 — Ownership and authority

1. Who is the named business owner accountable for this solution after go-live, and who is their backup?
2. Who has authority to approve scope changes and sign off on release?
3. When two departments disagree about process, who decides?
4. Who is the first point of contact when something breaks?

## Tier 1 — Records and retention

5. Is the output of this process an official record?
6. If yes, which system is the system of record, and what retention schedule applies?
7. Does this content fall under public-records, FOIA, legal-hold, or eDiscovery obligations?
8. If the content is also stored elsewhere, which copy is authoritative?

Ask these on any request touching enforcement, adjudication, finance, personnel, permits, or inspections — regardless of what the constraints field says.

## Tier 1 — Access and data

9. Who may view each category of data, and who may edit it?
10. Will anyone outside the organization need access?
11. Does this contain personal, financial, health, or employment-sensitive data?
12. Which identity groups already exist that we can use rather than creating new ones?
13. What is the approved data classification, and may this content enter the proposed recording, transcription, and AI services?

## Tier 1 — Scope boundary

14. What is explicitly out of scope for this phase?
15. Is there a date this must be operational by, and what happens if it is not?
16. Is this replacing an existing system, running alongside it, or new?
17. Does historical data need to come forward, and in what volume?

## Tier 1 — Licensing

18. Does any required capability depend on a premium connector or paid license?
19. Who approves recurring license cost, and is that approval already in place?

## Tier 1 — Discovery capture and AI

20. Have all participants received the required notice and consented to the approved recording/transcription method?
21. Who owns the recording, transcript, notes, prompt, and generated draft; where may each be stored; who may access each; and what retention/disposal rule applies?
22. Is the proposed AI service approved for this classification and content, and what content must be excluded or redacted?

---

## Tier 2 — Process

23. What are the stages a case moves through from creation to closure?
24. What triggers movement between stages, and who performs it?
25. What are the exception paths — rejection, reassignment, cancellation, reopening?
26. What happens when a deadline passes with no action?
27. Can the same subject have more than one case open at once?

## Tier 2 — Field work and devices

28. What devices, operating systems, orientations, and assistive technologies must be supported, and are the devices organization-managed?
29. Is connectivity reliable at the point of work?
30. If connectivity fails mid-task, what must happen to work in progress?
31. Is the work ever performed by someone who is not a licensed internal user?

## Tier 2 — Notifications

32. Who must be notified, on what event, and through which channel?
33. Is the notification informational, or does it require an action and a response?
34. What is the escalation path when nobody responds?
35. Is a digest acceptable, or is each event individually significant?

## Tier 2 — Media and documents

36. Are photos or documents required evidence, or supporting context?
37. Are they ever mandatory before a step can complete?
38. What file sizes and quantities are expected per case?
39. Must any document be generated for signature, mailing, or filing?

## Tier 2 — Volume

40. How many records exist today, how many are added per day/month/year, and what peak or seasonal spikes occur?
41. How many concurrent users at peak, and what response-time or processing-window expectations apply?
42. How long must records remain actively accessible before archive?

## Tier 2 — Support and operations

43. Who owns day-to-day support, who is the backup, and how do users request help?
44. What failures require alerting or escalation, to whom, and within what response target?
45. Who maintains business rules, reference data, licenses, connections, and service accounts?

---

## Tier 3 — Experience and reporting

46. What must a user see first when they open the solution?
47. Which views or filters matter most day to day?
48. What does leadership need to see, how often, and in what format?
49. What organizational accessibility baseline applies, and are there additional user needs for keyboard, screen reader, contrast, zoom/reflow, captions, language, or cognitive accessibility?
50. What naming and terminology does the client use that we should mirror?

---

## Question routing

| Topic | Route to |
|---|---|
| Ownership, approval, scope | Business owner and SDM |
| Records, retention, legal | Records officer, clerk, or legal |
| Access, identity, security | IT or security owner |
| Recording, transcription, AI boundary | Facilitator, privacy/security owner, records owner |
| Licensing and cost | IT and account owner |
| Process and exceptions | Operational subject-matter expert |
| Devices, connectivity, accessibility | IT, accessibility owner, and operational supervisor |
| Volume and performance | Operational supervisor and system owner |
| Support and escalation | Business owner and support owner |

## Recording answers

Every answer is recorded with a stable source ID, date, provider, and a source locator such as section, paragraph, or transcript timestamp. An unattributed answer is an assumption wearing a disguise.
