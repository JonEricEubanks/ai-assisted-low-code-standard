# Case Study A: Fictional Training Request Workflow

> Synthetic demonstration scenario. This is not a description of a pilot, client, organization, or deployed solution. All names, records, and outcomes are fictional.

## Problem and prior process

A fictional team receives training requests through unstructured messages and tracks follow-up in a shared list. Request details are sometimes incomplete, and staff repeat status checks.

## Constraints and assumptions

The demonstration uses synthetic users, courses, and requests in a disposable environment. It excludes real identities, tenant details, access findings, finance/records architecture, and external integrations. No production behavior is implied.

## Design and decisions

Use a simple request form, a structured synthetic request list, and a status view. Require the requester to confirm the course and preferred date. Keep approval and access decisions with a human owner. Do not automate eligibility decisions.

## Delivery and validation

The example follows intake, acceptance criteria, design review, implementation, deterministic checks, accessibility review, release checklist, and retrospective. A synthetic test submits a missing course; validation prevents submission and gives a clear correction message.

## Outcomes and evidence

No pilot result or realized benefit is claimed. A demonstration can show the intended status visibility, but a measured comparison would require a pre-defined baseline, comparable tasks, sample size, median, range, exclusions, source, and approval.

## Reuse and lessons

An independent-developer challenge is a proposed validation step, not a completed result. The reusable lesson is to define request fields and human decision points before building the form.
